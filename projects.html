Funded Research Projects

Optimizing Nanobody Sequence Design through Multi-Objective Engineering

Principal Investigator: Haibo Yang
Sponsor: National Institutes of Health, NIGMS
Award: R16GM159671
Project Period: September 2025 – June 2029

This project develops an AI-guided, multi-objective engineering framework for nanobody design. It jointly predicts and optimizes affinity, stability, selectivity, and solubility—properties that are essential to therapeutic development but often involve competing trade-offs. Predictive modeling is integrated with wet-lab validation through an iterative co-evolution loop, with the goal of reducing experimental trial and error and accelerating the development of nanobody therapeutics.

Distributed Optimization with Dimension-Free Communication: Theory and Algorithms

Principal Investigator: Haibo Yang
Sponsor: U.S. National Science Foundation
Award: 2537471
Project Period: September 2026 – August 2029

This project studies distributed optimization methods whose communication cost does not grow with model dimension. The research develops scalar-only communication frameworks, Hessian-informed acceleration methods, and participation-aware algorithms that remain robust when devices participate intermittently. The resulting methods are supported by theoretical analysis and evaluated through edge-computing and wireless-network testbeds, with applications to distributed large-language-model fine-tuning, mobile privacy analysis, and personalized recommendation.
